Two runs, two proof classes; neither is evidence for the other. Sixteen frozen scenarios, exhaustively enumerated - exact counts, not estimates. No exactly-once execution, no restart safety, no production readiness. ALLOW is a decision, not a verification. In this controlled corpus, per-target locking correctly coordinated what its keys represented, but missed hazards spanning distinct keys. With qualifying repository evidence present, Interlock changed that coordination; when the evidence was removed, the failures returned. Interlock makes that evidence part of the coordination decision before shared state changes.
